# References: Software Features and the Anatomy of Bloat

1. [Software bloat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_bloat) - Wikipedia - Defines bloat as code and features that consume resources without matching user value, with examples of growing install sizes. Gives this chapter its central vocabulary and its measurable footprint ideas.

2. [Feature creep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_creep) - Wikipedia - Explains how continuous addition of features beyond the original scope inflates complexity and schedules. Supports the chapter's account of how the gap between features and the user's job opens over time.

3. [Unix philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix_philosophy) - Wikipedia - Summarizes the design maxims of small, single-purpose programs that cooperate, from McIlroy to Raymond. Provides the contrast case of the single-purpose utility used throughout the chapter.

4. The Mythical Man-Month (Anniversary Edition) - Frederick P. Brooks Jr. - Addison-Wesley - Brooks named the second-system effect, where a designer's cautious first system is followed by an overloaded second one. His essays explain why feature accumulation feels reasonable at every step yet ends in bloat.

5. The UNIX Philosophy - Mike Gancarz - Digital Press - Gancarz distilled Unix design into nine tenets, including small is beautiful and make each program do one thing well. His short, quotable rules are the clearest statement of the lean alternative to bloat.

6. [Strategy Letter IV: Bloatware and the 80/20 Myth](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2001/03/23/strategy-letter-iv-bloatware-and-the-8020-myth/) - Joel on Software - Spolsky argues that every user needs a different 20 percent of features, so bloat is less irrational than it looks. A useful counterweight that this chapter's bloat claims must answer.

7. [A Plea for Lean Software](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1995/wirth.pdf) - Niklaus Wirth, IEEE Computer, 1995 - Wirth's classic essay shows how software grows slower and larger faster than hardware improves, and demonstrates a compact, lean system. Source of Wirth's law, quoted when the chapter discusses resource footprint.

8. [Worse Is Better](https://www.dreamsongs.com/WorseIsBetter.html) - Richard P. Gabriel - Gabriel contrasts simple, minimal designs with complete, feature-rich ones and explains why the simple ones often spread faster. Gives students a design-culture lens on why lean tools win and lose.

9. [The Art of Unix Programming: Basics of the Unix Philosophy](http://www.catb.org/esr/writings/taoup/html/ch01s06.html) - Eric S. Raymond - Lists seventeen design rules such as parsimony, modularity, and the rule of silence. Gives concrete, checkable criteria for judging whether a tool like ScreenGrab stays small.

10. [The suckless.org Philosophy](https://suckless.org/philosophy/) - suckless.org - Argues for software that is simple, minimal, and small enough to read fully, and names code bloat as the enemy. A modern, opinionated example of a community that practices the lean contrast case.
