# References: Total Cost of Ownership

1. [Total cost of ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_cost_of_ownership) - Wikipedia - Describes how purchase price, operation, maintenance, and disposal are summed over a stated horizon. The core method this chapter applies to software options.

2. [Software maintenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_maintenance) - Wikipedia - Classifies corrective, adaptive, perfective, and preventive maintenance and discusses their cost. Supports the chapter's claim that maintenance never ends and can exceed build cost.

3. [Break-even point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break-even_(economics)) - Wikipedia - Explains how to find the volume or time at which two cost curves cross. Gives the formula behind the chapter's break-even comparisons between buying and building.

4. Software Engineering Economics - Barry W. Boehm - Prentice Hall - Boehm introduced the COCOMO cost model and the first systematic economics of software, including maintenance effort. His work made lifecycle cost a calculable quantity instead of a guess.

5. Facts and Fallacies of Software Engineering - Robert L. Glass - Addison-Wesley - Glass collected evidence that maintenance typically consumes 40 to 80 percent of lifecycle cost, and presented it as blunt, citable facts. A readable source for the chapter's maintenance claims.

6. [Technical Debt](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TechnicalDebt.html) - Martin Fowler - Explains Ward Cunningham's metaphor of shortcuts accruing interest that future work must pay. Gives students a concrete way to think about the ongoing cost of owning code.

7. [Is High Quality Software Worth the Cost?](https://martinfowler.com/articles/is-quality-worth-cost.html) - Martin Fowler - Argues that internal quality pays back within weeks because it speeds later changes. Challenges the assumption that cheaper-now choices are cheaper overall.

8. [Things You Should Never Do, Part I](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2000/04/06/things-you-should-never-do-part-i/) - Joel on Software - Spolsky uses the Netscape rewrite to argue that discarding working code hides large costs. A cautionary case for the build side of the total-cost comparison.

9. [Technical Debt Quadrant](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TechnicalDebtQuadrant.html) - Martin Fowler - Sorts debt into deliberate or inadvertent and reckless or prudent. Helps students label the maintenance costs they include in a total cost of ownership table.

10. [Design Stamina Hypothesis](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/DesignStaminaHypothesis.html) - Martin Fowler - Argues that good design slows early progress slightly but wins after a short time as changes get cheaper. Explains why the cheapest option at purchase is rarely cheapest over a long horizon.
