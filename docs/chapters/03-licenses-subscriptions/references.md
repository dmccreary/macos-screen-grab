# References: Licenses, Subscriptions, and Vendor Incentives

1. [Software license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_license) - Wikipedia - Covers proprietary, free, and open-source licensing models and how a license governs use, copying, and redistribution. Supplies the legal baseline for per-computer and per-user pricing discussed in this chapter.

2. [Software as a service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_as_a_service) - Wikipedia - Describes subscription-delivered software, its pricing models, and its trade-offs for customers and vendors. Background for the recurring-revenue incentives the chapter analyzes.

3. [Vendor lock-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vendor_lock-in) - Wikipedia - Explains how switching costs, proprietary formats, and dependency tie customers to one vendor. Directly supports the chapter's treatment of file formats and exit costs.

4. Information Rules: A Strategic Guide to the Network Economy - Carl Shapiro and Hal R. Varian - Harvard Business School Press - Shapiro and Varian built the lock-in analysis that sums a customer's switching costs and shows vendors how to raise them. Their framework explains why subscriptions and proprietary formats are so profitable.

5. Free Software, Free Society - Richard M. Stallman - GNU Press - Stallman framed software freedom as four user rights: run, study, share, and modify. That ethical framing gives students a clear yardstick for judging what any license actually lets them do.

6. [What Is Free Software?](https://www.gnu.org/philosophy/free-sw.html) - GNU Project - Defines the four essential freedoms and explains why they matter to users. A primary-source counterpoint to the proprietary licensing models in this chapter.

7. [The Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd) - Open Source Initiative - Lists the ten criteria a license must meet to count as open source. Lets students separate truly open licenses from merely source-available ones.

8. [SPDX License List](https://spdx.org/licenses/) - Linux Foundation SPDX - Catalogs standard identifiers and full texts for hundreds of software licenses. A practical lookup when reading the license of a tool or choosing one for generated code.

9. [Choose a License](https://choosealicense.com/) - GitHub - Compares common open-source licenses in plain language with a permissions, conditions, and limitations summary. Helps students see how license choice changes what others may do with software.

10. [Various Licenses and Comments about Them](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list.html) - GNU Project - Reviews dozens of software licenses and says which grant the freedoms of free software. Lets students read how real license terms differ in what they allow.
