    Quiz: Licenses, Subscriptions, and Vendor Incentives¶

    Test your understanding of copyright, licenses, subscription pricing, vendor incentives, switching costs, and lock-in with these review questions.

    1. What does copyright give the author of a piece of software?¶

    1. Ownership of every idea the program implements
    2. The legal right to control who may copy, distribute, and adapt the work
    3. A guaranteed price for every copy sold
    4. Permission to use the program on any computer without a license
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Copyright is the legal right that gives the author of a creative work control over copying, distribution, and adaptation, and in most countries it arises automatically when the software is written. It protects the particular expression, meaning the code and designs, not the underlying idea. Nobody owns the idea of grabbing a region of the screen. Options A, C, and D claim rights that copyright does not grant.

    Concept Tested: Copyright

    See: Copyright and the Software License

    2. What is a software license?¶

    1. The program's compiled code
    2. A receipt that proves the buyer owns the source code
    3. The owner's underlying right to the work
    4. A legal agreement stating what the user may do with the software and under what conditions
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A license is the permission the copyright holder grants to a user, and it turns a program into something you can lawfully use. Copyright (option C) is the owner's underlying right, and the license is derived from it. Buying a license usually does not mean buying the software itself, so option B is wrong. The compiled code (option A) is the software, not the legal agreement around it.

    Concept Tested: Software License

    See: Copyright and the Software License

    3. What does a perpetual license usually give the buyer?¶

    1. The right to keep using the version they bought indefinitely, but not necessarily future versions, updates, or support
    2. The right to receive every future version at no charge
    3. The right to modify and resell the source code
    4. The right to use the software only while paying a recurring fee
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A perpetual license has no expiry date, so the version bought keeps working as long as the operating system runs it. It does not usually include later versions, updates, or support, and the vendor may charge for the next major version. Option B overstates the grant. Option C describes open-source licenses, and option D describes a subscription license.

    Concept Tested: Perpetual License

    See: One-Time and Perpetual Licenses

    4. What are switching costs?¶

    1. The fees a vendor pays to change its licensing model
    2. The price difference between a monthly and an annual plan
    3. The costs a customer bears to move from one product to another, including money, time, effort, and risk
    4. The cost of upgrading from one version to the next
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Switching costs can be small, such as downloading another app, or large, such as retraining a team and converting ten years of files. They matter to vendors because a customer facing a high switching cost will tolerate a price increase that a free-to-leave customer would not. Options A, B, and D describe other costs that do not capture the customer's cost of leaving.

    Concept Tested: Switching Costs

    See: Switching Costs, File Formats, and Lock-In

    5. Why can a vendor lawfully change future terms or charge "per computer" or "per year"?¶

    1. Because the buyer owns the software but has agreed to rent it back
    2. Because license terms never change after purchase
    3. Because copyright expires once the software is sold
    4. Because the buyer usually purchases permission to use a copy on the owner's terms, not the software itself
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter separates copyright, the owner's right, from the license, the permission granted, and from ownership of a copy. A buyer typically holds a permission slip from the copyright holder, and the holder decides what the slip says. That is why per-computer or per-year terms are perfectly lawful. Option A reverses the relationship, option B is false, and option C is wrong because copyright does not expire at sale.

    Concept Tested: Software License

    See: Copyright and the Software License

    6. What happens to a subscription license when the user stops paying?¶

    1. The permission to use the software ends, and the product may stop launching, refuse to open files, or fall back to a restricted mode
    2. The user keeps full rights to the latest version forever
    3. The user receives a refund for all earlier payments
    4. The vendor must release the source code
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A subscription grants the right to use the software only while the fee is paid, which reverses the logic of the one-time license: you rent access instead of buying a version. What happens next varies by product, and it is worth asking before subscribing. In the book's case, someone who pays for three years and stops has spent $270 and holds no right to run the tool, while the $9 buyer still has a working program.

    Concept Tested: Subscription License

    See: Subscriptions and Annual Fees

    7. Using the formula C = n × f × y, what is the total cost of a per-computer subscription for 4 computers at $30 per computer per year over 3 years?¶

    1. $120
    2. $360
    3. $270
    4. $480
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Multiply the number of computers (4), the annual fee per computer ($30), and the number of years (3): 4 × $30 × 3 = $360. Option A is one year for four computers, option C matches the author's three computers over three years, and option D would be four computers for four years. The same formula gives the book's $450 for three computers over five years.

    Concept Tested: Annual Fee

    See: Subscriptions and Annual Fees

    8. A single person owns three computers, and a tool costs $30 whether counted per computer or per user. Which licensing model is cheaper for this person, and by how much?¶

    1. Per computer, by $60
    2. They cost the same
    3. Per user, by $60
    4. Per user, by $30
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Per-computer licensing costs 3 × $30 = $90, while per-user licensing costs one person × $30 = $30, a saving of $60. For an individual with several machines, per-user pricing is usually cheaper. For three people with one computer each, the two models both cost $90, and for larger groups the arithmetic can reverse, so always count what the license counts.

    Concept Tested: Per-User Licensing

    See: Counting Licenses: Per Computer and Per User

    9. How does moving from a one-time license to a subscription change a vendor's strongest incentives?¶

    1. The vendor no longer cares about customers after the sale
    2. The vendor focuses on winning new buyers and persuading old ones to upgrade
    3. The vendor stops charging for new features
    4. The vendor focuses on keeping customers from canceling and on raising the fee they pay
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Under a one-time license, incentives center on winning new buyers and selling upgrades to old ones, and they largely stop at the sale. Under a subscription, the vendor earns only as long as the customer keeps paying, so retention and the renewal price dominate decisions. Option B describes the one-time model. The chapter notes that honest engineers and managers are still steered by the revenue model.

    Concept Tested: Vendor Incentives

    See: How Pricing Shapes Vendor Behavior

    10. A user has 200 files in a vendor's proprietary format. Each takes 1 minute to convert, their time is worth $40 per hour, and learning a replacement takes 4 hours. If switching saves $90 per year in fees, about how long does it take to recover the switching cost?¶

    1. About 1 year
    2. About 3.3 years
    3. About 6 years
    4. It can never be recovered
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Conversion takes 200 ÷ 60 = 3.33 hours, which at $40 costs $133.33. Learning costs 4 × $40 = $160. The total switching cost is $293.33, and dividing by the $90 annual saving gives about 3.26 years. A vendor that knows this can raise the fee a little each year without losing the customer. An open format such as PNG would have made the conversion cost zero.
